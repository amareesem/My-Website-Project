The 5-Minute Brain Workout Hiding Inside Gym Class

Cross-lateral movement strengthens the bridge between your brain hemispheres, sharpening memory, focus, and balance. Try these 3 moves in 5–7 minutes.

Lori King, MD · 2026-10-12 · 3 min read

Zulu dancers, Finnish schoolchildren, and South American shamanic ceremonies all instinctively use one specific type of movement - crossing one side of the body over the midline. It turns out to be some of the most neurologically efficient brain training available, and it takes five minutes.

Zulu dancers, South American shamanic ceremonies, Finnish schoolchildren - what do they all have in common? They all instinctively use cross-lateral movement: crossing one side of the body over the midline to engage the other side.

Turns out this isn't just cultural tradition, it's some of the most neurologically efficient brain training you can do. And all it takes is 5-7 minutes.

Here's why it matters so much:

  • Sharper Thinking: Boosts cognitive flexibility, processing speed, and executive function.
  • Better Memory: Strengthens communication between brain hemispheres, reinforcing learning and recall.
  • Fall Prevention: Sharpens balance and body coordination - a literal life-saver as we age.

Pearl of the Week: You have a bridge in your brain. These moves strengthen it.

The corpus callosum is a thick bundle of nerve fibers connecting your left and right brain hemispheres. The left brain handles logic and language; the right handles creativity and spatial awareness. Cross-lateral movement is one of the only things you can do to actively strengthen that bridge, which is why physical therapists use it in stroke rehab and teachers use it in classrooms. Now you can use it in your living room.

Three moves to start with today - no equipment, no gym, no excuses:

Three Moves To Try

  • Windmill Taps - Feet wide, right hand to left foot, left hand to right foot. 1 minute.
  • Cross-Crawl March - March in place, right elbow to left knee, left elbow to right knee. 1–2 minutes.
  • Figure-8 Tracing - Use your hand or eyes to trace large figure-8s horizontally in the air. Integrates visual and motor coordination - and looks just mysterious enough to intrigue your family.

Just 5-7 minutes is enough. Tack these onto your morning routine, your walk, or your post-workout stretch. You can even do the cross-crawl march while your coffee brews. Small investment, remarkable return, especially over years and decades of consistent practice.

Cross-lateral movement isn't flashy. It won't go viral on fitness TikTok. But it is one of the most neurologically efficient things you can do to protect your memory, sharpen your focus, and keep your body safe as you age. Your brain is a two-hemisphere operation - give both sides something to work with.

Now go march in place and pretend you're doing something very sophisticated.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is cross-lateral movement?

Cross-lateral movement means crossing one side of your body over the midline to engage the other side, such as touching your right elbow to your left knee. It is used in stroke rehab, classrooms, and traditional dance around the world.

How does cross-lateral movement help the brain?

It strengthens the corpus callosum, the bundle of nerve fibers connecting your left and right brain hemispheres. That supports cognitive flexibility, processing speed, memory, and balance.

How long should I do cross-lateral exercises?

Just 5 to 7 minutes is enough. Add windmill taps, the cross-crawl march, or figure-8 tracing to your morning routine, your walk, or your post-workout stretch.
