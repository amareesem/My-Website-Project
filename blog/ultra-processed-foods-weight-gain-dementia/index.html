Ultra-Processed Foods: How They Drive Weight Gain, Disease, and Dementia Risk (and How to Cut Them)

Ultra-processed foods add about 500 calories a day and are linked to dementia and heart disease. Learn how to spot them and cut them for good.

Lori King, MD · 2026-09-01 · 9 min read

Ultra-processed foods are industrial products made mostly from refined ingredients and additives, like chips, packaged snacks, sweetened cereals, frozen dinners, and most protein bars. They’re engineered to be easy to overeat: in an NIH trial, people ate about 500 extra calories a day on an ultra-processed diet and gained weight. They’re also linked to heart disease, type 2 diabetes, depression, faster cognitive decline, and a higher risk of dementia. The fix is simple: follow a 5-ingredient rule and shop the perimeter of the grocery store.

You’ve probably looked at your pantry and wondered if aliens have been stocking it while you sleep. Let’s talk about what these foods are doing to your body, and how to take your kitchen back.

Key Takeaways

  • On an ultra-processed diet, people ate about 500 more calories a day and gained about 2 pounds in 2 weeks. On an unprocessed diet, they lost about 2 pounds.
  • Ultra-processed food is linked to 32 different health harms across studies of nearly 10 million people.
  • Every 10% increase in ultra-processed food was linked to a 25% higher dementia risk.
  • Not every packaged food is ultra-processed: frozen vegetables, canned beans, plain nuts, and olive oil are fine.
  • The 5-ingredient rule and a 2-week perimeter shopping challenge make the switch easy.

What Are Ultra-Processed Foods?

Scientists sort foods by how much they’ve been processed. Ultra-processed foods are the real villains: industrial creations made mostly from refined ingredients and additives you’d never find in a home kitchen. Think chips, packaged snacks, sodas, sweetened cereals, frozen dinners, and most “protein bars.”

Plenty of good foods come in a package, though. Frozen vegetables, canned beans, plain nuts, olive oil, and spices are minimally processed and welcome. The goal is cutting the ultra-processed stuff and learning to tell the difference.

Why Ultra-Processed Foods Are So Hard to Stop Eating

They’re engineered to be irresistible. Food companies spend millions finding the exact combination of salt, sugar, and fat that hits your brain’s “bliss point.” Researchers using a validated food addiction scale estimate that about 1 in 7 adults meet the criteria for addiction to ultra-processed foods. It’s not a lack of willpower. It’s engineering.

These foods are soft, calorie-dense, and fast to swallow, so they slip past your fullness signals. It’s like locking leptin, your “I’m full” hormone, in a soundproof room while your brain keeps hearing “KEEP EATING.”

One Package, Three Battles

Ultra-processed foods and weight gain

The clearest proof comes from a 2019 NIH study. The same people ate an ultra-processed diet for two weeks and an unprocessed diet for two weeks. Meals were matched for sugar, fat, and fiber, and they could eat as much as they wanted.

  • Ultra-processed diet: about 500 extra calories a day, eaten faster, and about 2 pounds gained.
  • Unprocessed diet: about 2 pounds lost, without trying or counting. A hormone that helps you feel full went up, and the hunger hormone went down.

Same person, different food, completely different outcome.

Ultra-processed foods and chronic disease

A 2024 umbrella review in the BMJ, covering nearly 10 million people, linked higher ultra-processed food intake to 32 health harms. The strongest evidence was for death from heart disease, type 2 diabetes, and anxiety and depression. The World Health Organization’s cancer agency also classifies processed meats (bacon, ham, hot dogs, deli meats) as a known cause of colorectal cancer.

Ultra-processed foods and dementia

  • Higher dementia risk. In a UK study of more than 72,000 adults, every 10% increase in ultra-processed food was linked to a 25% higher dementia risk. Swapping just 10% of those calories for unprocessed foods was estimated to lower risk by about 19%.
  • Faster decline. In a Brazilian study of more than 10,000 adults, people who got over 20% of calories from ultra-processed foods had a 28% faster rate of cognitive decline over about eight years.
  • Stroke and memory. A 2024 U.S. study linked more ultra-processed food to a higher risk of both stroke and cognitive impairment.

These are observational studies, so they show strong links, not proof. But the pattern is hard to ignore: the same foods that expand your waistline and harm your heart show up again and again in brain research.

What’s Really in Processed Food?

  • Preservatives. BHA, still allowed in U.S. foods, is listed by the National Toxicology Program as “reasonably anticipated to be a human carcinogen” based on animal studies. Sodium benzoate plus vitamin C in drinks can form small amounts of benzene.
  • Artificial colors. A landmark Lancet study found certain artificial color mixes increased hyperactivity in some children. The FDA banned Red No. 3 from food in January 2025 and announced plans to phase out petroleum-based synthetic dyes.
  • Emulsifiers. In mice, common emulsifiers thinned the gut’s protective mucus layer and triggered inflammation and weight gain. In people, one emulsifier disrupted healthy gut bacteria, and in a large French study, higher emulsifier intake was linked to more heart disease. Human research is young, but your gut lining isn’t something to gamble with.
  • “Natural flavors.” A single “natural flavor” can be a blend of many components, including solvents and preservatives, and none have to be listed.

How Ultra-Processed Food Blocks Weight Loss

  1. You eat more without realizing it. Calorie-dense, low-fiber, low-protein, fast-to-eat foods let hundreds of extra calories sneak in before fullness catches up.
  2. Inflammation jams your hormones. Diets heavy in ultra-processed foods are linked to higher inflammatory markers, which make it harder for insulin and leptin to work (more on that in how to quit sugar).
  3. Your gut gets out of balance. Low fiber and additives can starve and disrupt the bacteria that help regulate weight and inflammation.
  4. Packaging chemicals may play a role. Some chemicals that leach from packaging, like BPA and certain phthalates, are studied as “obesogens” because they can nudge fat cells to grow in lab and animal studies. Human research is still catching up.

How to Cut Ultra-Processed Foods: The 5-Ingredient Rule

If it has more than 5 ingredients, or ingredients you can’t pronounce, it doesn’t go in your cart. An apple has one ingredient: apple. (A few good guys have scary names. “Ascorbic acid” is just vitamin C. When in doubt, scan it with an app like Bobby Approved.)

Your pantry purge list

  • Chips and crackers (even “healthy” baked ones)
  • Cookies and baked goods
  • Frozen meals and freezer pizzas
  • Canned soups (usually sodium bombs)
  • Deli meats
  • Breakfast cereals, granola bars, and protein bars
  • Packaged snacks that last for months
  • Boxed meals (“just add water”)
  • Bottled salad dressings and sugary condiments like ketchup, BBQ, and teriyaki sauce

The 2-week perimeter challenge

For two weeks, shop only the perimeter of the grocery store, where real food lives: produce, meat, fish, and eggs. Venture into the center aisles only for single-ingredient items: olive oil, vinegar, herbs and spices, no-salt-added canned beans (BPA-free when you can), raw unsalted nuts and seeds, and plain frozen vegetables and fruit.

  • Week 1: Purge the pantry, shop the perimeter, prep vegetables and grains in bulk, and keep meals simple (grilled protein, roasted vegetables, salad).
  • Week 2: Master a few quick go-to meals, batch-cook soups and stews, and note your energy, sleep, digestion, and focus.

Too tired to cook? Pre-washed greens, avocado, rotisserie chicken (check the ingredients and sodium), olive oil, and lemon: dinner in 3 minutes. Need a snack now? Apple slices with almond butter, raw nuts, or carrots with hummus.

Safety check: Most sodium in the American diet comes from processed and restaurant food, so this change can lower your blood pressure quickly. If you take blood pressure medication, check your pressure at home, watch for dizziness when standing, and talk with your physician about your dose. Cutting refined carbs can also lower blood sugar, so if you take insulin or other diabetes medications that can cause lows, check your levels more often.

What Happens When You Stop Eating Processed Food

  • You naturally eat less, about 500 fewer calories a day in the NIH study
  • Appetite hormones get back on your side
  • Sodium drops, often lowering blood pressure and protecting the tiny blood vessels in your brain
  • Fiber climbs, feeding gut bacteria that regulate weight, blood sugar, and inflammation
  • Your additive load plummets

Many patients notice less bloating, steadier energy, better sleep, and clearer thinking within about two weeks. By week 3, cravings drop and weight loss gets noticeably easier.

Recipe: “I Can’t Believe It’s Not Processed” Vegetable Soup

Ingredients: 2 tablespoons olive oil, 1 diced onion, 3 chopped carrots, 3 chopped celery stalks, 3 minced garlic cloves, 8 cups low-sodium vegetable broth, 1 can or jar diced tomatoes (no sugar added), 2 cups chopped organic kale, 1 cup white beans, 1 teaspoon each dried oregano and basil, and salt and pepper.

Instructions:

  1. Heat the oil in a large pot and sauté the onion, carrots, and celery for about 5 minutes.
  2. Add the garlic and cook 1 minute more.
  3. Add the broth, tomatoes, and herbs, and bring to a boil.
  4. Simmer 15 minutes, then add the kale and beans and simmer 5 more.
  5. Season, then freeze extra portions for busy days.

Finish each bowl with fresh lemon and parsley for flavor without more salt. If you take warfarin, keep your kale intake consistent from day to day.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between processed and ultra-processed food?

Minimally processed foods, like frozen vegetables, canned beans, plain nuts, and olive oil, are close to their natural form. Ultra-processed foods are industrial products made mostly from refined ingredients and additives, like chips, sweetened cereals, packaged snacks, and frozen dinners.

Do ultra-processed foods cause weight gain?

In a controlled NIH trial, people ate about 500 more calories a day and gained about 2 pounds in two weeks on an ultra-processed diet, then lost about 2 pounds on an unprocessed diet, even though they could eat as much as they wanted on both.

Are ultra-processed foods linked to dementia?

Yes, in observational studies. A UK study of more than 72,000 adults linked every 10% increase in ultra-processed food to a 25% higher dementia risk, and a Brazilian study linked high intake to 28% faster cognitive decline.

What is the 5-ingredient rule?

It's a simple shopping guide: if a product has more than 5 ingredients, or ingredients you can't pronounce, leave it on the shelf. It quickly filters out most ultra-processed foods.

What foods should I eat instead of processed foods?

Shop the grocery store perimeter for produce, meat, fish, and eggs, and add single-ingredient staples like olive oil, spices, no-salt-added canned beans, raw nuts, and plain frozen vegetables and fruit.
