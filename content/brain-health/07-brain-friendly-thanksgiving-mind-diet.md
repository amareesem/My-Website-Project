Thanksgiving is a hard week to talk about food without sliding into guilt or hype. So let's skip both. You can enjoy the table and still lean toward foods that have a good track record for heart and brain health: leafy greens, berries, nuts, olive oil, and fish. That pattern has a name, the MIND diet, and it deserves an honest telling, especially if you are gathering with family and thinking about a parent or grandparent who had dementia.

The encouraging news comes mostly from observational studies, in which researchers watch what people already eat. The more cautious news comes from a 2023 randomized trial. In that three-year study, both the MIND diet group and the control group improved, and there was no significant difference between them. Both groups also lost weight. That does not make the MIND pattern a failure. It does mean we should not sell it as a proven, stand-alone way to protect memory.

## Key Takeaways

- The MIND diet blends the Mediterranean diet and the DASH diet. It emphasizes leafy greens, berries, nuts, whole grains, olive oil, fish, and poultry, and it limits red meat, butter, cheese, sweets, and fried food.
- Earlier observational studies associated the MIND pattern with slower cognitive decline.
- In a 2023 three-year randomized trial of 604 older adults, both the MIND group and a calorie-reduced control group improved, with no significant difference between them. Global cognition rose by 0.205 standardized units in the MIND group and 0.170 in the control group (mean difference, 0.035; 95% CI, -0.022 to 0.092; P = 0.23).
- The MIND group lost 5.0 kilograms (about 11 pounds) and the control group lost 4.8 kilograms (also about 11 pounds). Both received a lot of counseling and support. The paper suggests the control group may also have improved their diet, and that practice effects on repeated testing may help explain why both groups improved.
- You can still build a holiday plate around MIND-style foods without turning dinner into a test.

## What the MIND Diet Is

MIND stands for Mediterranean-DASH Intervention for Neurodegenerative Delay. In plain words, it is a mix of two well-studied eating patterns: the Mediterranean diet and the DASH diet, which was designed to help blood pressure. Researchers at Rush University, including the late Martha Clare Morris, shaped MIND around foods most often linked with brain health.

A MIND-style plate leans on:

- Green leafy vegetables, berries, nuts, whole grains, olive oil, and fish
- Poultry and beans as regular proteins
- Less red meat, butter and stick margarine, full-fat cheese, pastries and sweets, and fried food

You do not have to follow it perfectly for it to be useful. Think of it as a pattern, not a scorecard for a single Thursday.

## What the 2023 Trial Actually Found

The trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine, enrolled 604 people who were overweight, had a less-than-ideal diet, and had a family history of Alzheimer's disease. They were 65 or older and did not have cognitive impairment at the start. One group was coached to follow the MIND diet. The other group followed a usual diet with mild calorie reduction. Both groups were asked to cut about 250 calories a day, and both got a lot of support from dietitians.

After three years, there was no significant statistical difference in the change in cognition between the two groups. Global cognition scores rose by 0.205 standardized units in the MIND group and by 0.170 in the control group (mean difference, 0.035; 95% CI, -0.022 to 0.092; P = 0.23). The MIND group lost 5.0 kilograms (95% CI, -5.8 to -4.1), or about 11 pounds. The control group lost 4.8 kilograms (95% CI, -5.7 to -4.0), also about 11 pounds. The authors note that the control group, who were also asked to cut calories, probably improved their diet as well, given the similar weight loss, and that practice effects from repeated cognitive testing could help explain why both groups improved.

This was a careful study with a quieter headline than earlier observational papers. We can keep the useful eating pattern and drop the oversell.

## How MIND Fits With a Bigger Lifestyle Picture

Diet is one piece of brain health, not the whole picture. In the U.S. POINTER trial, the MIND diet was one part of a broader program that also included exercise, mental and social challenge, and health monitoring. That is different from testing a diet by itself. The [Alzheimer's Association POINTER summary](https://www.alz.org/us-pointer/study-results) has those results.

## A Thanksgiving Plate Without the Guilt

You do not have to redesign the whole menu. A few easy shifts still look like a feast:

- Put a generous pile of greens on the plate, as a salad or a side of cooked greens, before the heavier dishes.
- Add color with roasted vegetables or a berry dish.
- Set out a bowl of nuts for snacking, instead of only sweets.
- Use olive oil on vegetables or a salad.
- If fish fits your table, it is a MIND-style win. If turkey is the centerpiece, that works too. Poultry is part of the pattern.
- Enjoy the traditional dishes you love. The pattern is about what you do most of the time, not about a single slice of pie.

If dementia has touched your family, a meal like this can also be a quiet way of caring for one another: good food, shared time, no lecture at the table.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the MIND diet?

It is an eating pattern that mixes the Mediterranean diet and the DASH diet, with a focus on foods linked to brain health, such as leafy greens, berries, nuts, olive oil, and fish.

### Did the MIND diet fail its big trial?

Not exactly. In the 2023 three-year trial, people on the MIND diet improved, and so did people in the control group. There was no significant difference between them. Both groups lost weight and received a lot of support. The result is a reason to be modest, not a reason to give up on vegetables and fish.

### Can I follow a brain-friendly pattern on Thanksgiving?

Yes. Add greens, colorful vegetables, berries, nuts, and olive oil to the table, include fish or poultry, and enjoy the traditional foods you love. One meal does not make or break a pattern.

### Is the MIND diet the same as what U.S. POINTER used?

U.S. POINTER included the MIND diet as one part of a larger lifestyle program, along with exercise, mental and social activity, and health monitoring. That is different from testing the diet by itself.
